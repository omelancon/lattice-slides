# Implementation report: checkpoints for fast navigation (Lattice 0.27.0, 2026-10-07)

Two quick presses of Shift+Right used to need three presses within a second and went to the last step of the slide, which is too far on a slide that shows several things in turn (bullets, then a CFG, then an arrow walking over it). Two presses within half a second now play to the next **checkpoint**, the end of the current run of steps that move the same tracks, and two presses of Shift+Left play back to the previous one. Checkpoints are computed at build time from the step table. End keeps going to the last step; Home now goes to the first step of the slide, and the start of the deck moved to Shift+Home. The rules are in spec 6.5 (checkpoints) and 7.6 (two quick presses), with 1, 4, 7.2, 7.5, 11.2 and 15; the rationale is in design report 3.12 and decision 37 (decision 32 amended). The design and Olivier's answers are in the project document `claude/v0.27-design.md`.

## 1. What was built

- **Checkpoints** (`timeline.py`, `checkpoints`, called at the end of `compile_steps` after the `at=` insertions). The kind of a step is the set of tracks whose position changes (independent tracks and the columns track; followers never count). Steps that change nothing (detour steps, LT030 cues) belong to no run; a blocking detour step ends the run before it. The checkpoints are the last step of each run and the last step of the slide. `Slide.checkpoints` (`model.py`); the deck JSON carries `checkpoints` only when one precedes the last step (`emit.py`), so most slides produce the same JSON as before.
- **Bindings** (`model.py`): `first-step` on `Home`, listed before `home` so that a deck whose front matter still binds `home: Home` keeps it (the later action wins in `keyMap`); `home` on `Shift+Home`.
- **Runtime** (`lattice.js`):
  - `checkpointsOf`, `nextCheckpoint`, `prevCheckpoint` (a lookup in the build's list);
  - `burstMove` replaces `burstTarget`: two presses of the same skip action within 500 ms (`BURST_WINDOW_MS`, from the first press) play to the checkpoint counted from the **anchor**, the step before the first press, or the target of a checkpoint playback still under way in the same direction (so two double presses in a row go two checkpoints ahead). Later presses in the window keep the target; auto-repeat does not count; another action or a slide key restarts the count, as before;
  - the **hold**: the first press plays its ten steps from the anchor, but `playSteps(target, {hold})` waits at the checkpoint until the window closes, so a second press never plays back over steps it has just played;
  - `playSteps` keeps a record of the playback under way (`playing`), which `burstMove` reads before `onKey` stops it;
  - `actions["first-step"]` (`playSteps(0)`); the Keybindings section lists it, and its "×2" row reads "next or previous checkpoint (two presses within half a second)".
- **Docs**: spec (above); README (Navigation table); `docs/USER_SKILL.md` (the keys row, a checkpoints row, the snapshot note on `Home`, and a "Rules that bite" line on combined cues); `docs/SKILL.md` (structure lines of `timeline.py` and `lattice.js`, the test table); design report (3.12, roadmap v0.27, decisions 32 and 37); todo; the manual's `presenting-keys` table and a new slide "Checkpoints: one animation at a time" (`checkpoints`: two bullets, a bubble sort, an arrow's three moves, the rest of the list; checkpoints 2, 10, 13 and 16); the 0.26 report copied to `docs/archive/` and listed in its README; version 0.27.0.

## 2. Decisions taken with Olivier (design document, section 13)

A double Shift+Left lands on the previous checkpoint (a round trip with a double Shift+Right); kinds are per track; a step moving several tracks is a kind of its own; non-blocking detour steps are passed and blocking ones end a run; the first press holds at the checkpoint; later presses keep the target; a 500 ms window with a lone press still a ten-step skip; End still stops in front of a blocking detour step and Home plays back to step 0; a new manual slide.

One refinement during the work: a lone press made while a checkpoint playback is still running plays its ten steps from that playback's target (its anchor) rather than from the current step, so a press after the window never cuts a checkpoint playback short. Without a playback under way the anchor is the current step, as before (spec 7.6).
